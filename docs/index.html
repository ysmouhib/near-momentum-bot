An honest account of what a trend engine can and cannot prove.

This project exists to demonstrate cost-aware backtesting and walk-forward validation done honestly — including the part where the honest answer is "not distinguishable from noise." Its one robust finding: on 91 days of real data, the v1 4-minute scalper lost 82% out-of-sample — the edge never survived 0.24% round-trip costs. v2 is the disciplined response: a slower, volatility-targeted trend engine, evaluated with the statistical caution the small sample demands. On six years of data across four symbols, the honest answer stayed the same — the multi-year verdict.

Validation discipline, not a performance claim

Read this as a worked example of honest evaluation, not as evidence that this strategy makes money. The 91-day sample below is now the trailer, not the movie: far too small to conclude anything on its own, it stays here to show how to see that, rather than hide it. The movie — six years, four symbols — follows the caveats.

Setup: 91 days of real NEARUSDT 1-minute data (mid-April → mid-July 2026), aggregated to 60-minute bars, with Binance taker fees (0.10%/side) and slippage (0.02%/side) charged on every fill. Walk-forward re-optimises a deliberately small grid on rolling 30-day windows and reports only the unseen 10-day windows that follow.

Read the +10.7% row together with the caveats below — on this sample it is one draw from a wide distribution, not evidence of skill. Every figure is reproducible in two commands (see Quickstart), including the ones that undercut the strategy.

The numbers above are statistically inconclusive, on purpose

  • The trade count is inflated. 38 "trades" are the legs of a top-3 parameter ensemble over the same windows, so they are heavily correlated — the number of independent decisions is closer to ~13. A Sharpe of 2.84 estimated on ~13 effective, serially-dependent observations has a standard error wide enough that the 95% confidence interval comfortably contains zero.
  • The 2× cost stress going up is a warning, not a win. Costs cannot improve any individual trade; the rise from +10.7% to +13.3% means the in-sample optimiser simply selected different parameters under stressed costs — a direct measurement of selection noise, reported instead of buried.
  • One market phase. 91 days is a single trending regime for NEAR. ETH (+5.8%) and especially SOL (+0.3% — i.e. nothing) on the same window are the honest counter-evidence, deliberately left in.
  • Buy & hold beat it on raw return (+38.2% over the same windows) with far larger drawdowns; the strategy was invested only ~30% of the time.

Six years later: the multi-year verdict

The caveats above named what a real claim would need: multi-year data, bootstrapped confidence intervals and a deflated Sharpe ratio. That run is done — NEAR, ETH, SOL and BTC 1-minute klines from 2021-01-01 to 2026-09-30 (SHA-256-verified Binance dumps), walk-forward on two window sizes, 10,000-resample block-bootstrap confidence intervals, independent decisions counted, and a deflated Sharpe charged against the trials ledger. All ten symbol × window blocks: not statistically distinguishable from noise.

The obvious next question — could better parameters rescue it? — got a pre-registered answer: 125 configurations on 2021–2024 (NEAR/ETH/SOL), with the grid, the selection rule and the trials-ledger charge committed before any configuration was scored. It selected the corner cell theta_in=0.15, trail_atr=4.0, er_min=0.1: in-sample Sharpe +1.13 [95% CI +0.15, +2.07], but a deflated-Sharpe probability of 0.69 against the required 0.95 after charging for 219 configurations — not established. The corner is the tell: the search converged toward the loosest filters, i.e. toward more time in the market, and on the same bars it trails plain buy-and-hold.

The holdout (2025-01-01 → 2026-09-30) was deliberately not fired: a selection that fails its own pre-registered gate does not get to spend it. It stays sealed for a future strategy family — as a viewed holdout, not a pristine one, since those months are part of the validation run above.

The conclusion actually defended

The v1 scalper is decisively dead (−82% out-of-sample, a robust finding). v2 is not distinguishable from noise — first on 91 days, then on six years across four symbols, with the bootstrapped confidence intervals and deflated Sharpe the 91-day sample lacked. A pre-registered search for better parameters converged toward market exposure and trails buy-and-hold.

The deliverable here is the machinery — and the discipline to reach that conclusion honestly — not the +10.7%.

What changed, and why it matters

v1 AND-ed six binary conditions into a 4-minute entry signal. Binary gates are brittle (nudge one threshold and trades flip on/off — the signature of curve-fitting), and the 4-minute timeframe made fees mathematically unbeatable. v2 keeps the two good ideas (order flow from taker-buy volume, walk-forward discipline) and rebuilds everything else:

1. A score, not a checklist

Conviction is a continuous blend in [−1, 1]: volatility-normalised time-series momentum (fast + slow), Donchian breakout position, and smoothed taker-imbalance order flow. Signal strength now means something, and parameters move results smoothly instead of flipping individual trades.

2. Regime gates

A Kaufman efficiency-ratio filter refuses to trade chop, and a 200-bar trend filter refuses to buy downtrends. On 90 days of real data the trend filter alone moved a falling asset (SOL) from −5.9% to +0.3% out-of-sample: you cannot buy your way out of a downtrend, but you can decline to try.

3. Hysteresis — the cost-aware band

Enter when the score crosses θin, exit when it fades below θout < θin. The band between them is the no-trade region where expected edge is smaller than round-trip cost. This is where most scalping systems donate their P&L to the exchange.

4. Volatility-targeted sizing

Position weight = target annualised vol ÷ realised vol, capped at 100%. Quiet markets get bigger positions, wild markets smaller ones — risk stays constant across regimes, which is what makes risk-adjusted comparisons between periods meaningful.

5. A timeframe where costs stop being decisive

Costs are fixed per trade; trends scale with time. At 4 minutes the average move is a rounding error next to 0.24% round-trip costs; at 60 minutes it isn't. The default grid searches slower bars, not faster ones.

6. Top-k walk-forward ensemble

Instead of trading the single best in-sample parameter set (mostly luck), each window trades the top-3 and averages them — selection variance traded away. On this sample it doubled the OOS trade count while raising OOS Sharpe; the mechanism matters more than the number.

Full reasoning, the failure analysis of v1, the plateau-robustness table and the named statistical gaps are in docs/STRATEGY.md and the roadmap.

Reproduce every number

git clone https://github.com/ysmouhib/near-momentum-bot.git
cd near-momentum-bot
pip install -e ".[dev,plot]"

# 1. Download a fixed window of real 1m klines (no API key; Binance public dumps)
python scripts/download_data.py --start 2026-04-17 --end 2026-07-17

# 2. Fixed-config backtest (optimistic upper bound)
near-bot backtest --csv data/NEARUSDT_1m_20260417_20260717.csv --trades --plot reports/backtest.png

# 3. Walk-forward validation (the number that actually matters)
near-bot walkforward --csv data/NEARUSDT_1m_20260417_20260717.csv --plot reports/oos.png

# 4. The validation battery over every downloaded symbol of the window
#    (bootstrap CIs, deflated Sharpe, independent decisions, verdict)
python scripts/download_data.py --symbols NEARUSDT ETHUSDT SOLUSDT BTCUSDT --start 2026-04-17 --end 2026-07-17
near-bot validate --start 2026-04-17 --end 2026-07-17 --windows 30:10   # -> reports/validate.md
#    (91 days fit only the 30d/10d variant; the default also runs 90d/30d)

# 5. (Optional) paper trade on the testnet — the overlay starts DISARMED
cp .env.example .env   # paste testnet keys from testnet.binance.vision
export $(cat .env | xargs)
near-bot test-connection
near-bot arm --note "why a directional move is plausible now"
near-bot status        # overlay state + BTC/ETH regime context
near-bot paper

Or skip the terminal entirely: the browser simulator runs the same engine (parity-checked trade-for-trade against the Python code) on live Binance data, with every parameter adjustable — including walk-forward validation in-page.
